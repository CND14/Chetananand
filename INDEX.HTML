Mutual Funds

Mutual funds pool money from various investors to purchase a diversified portfolio of stocks, bonds, or other securities, managed by professional fund managers.

Equity Funds

Investments primarily in stocks. Suitable for long-term horizons and those willing to accept market volatility for potential capital appreciation.

Debt Funds

Investments in fixed-income securities like government bonds and corporate debentures. Typically chosen for stability and regular income generation.

Hybrid Funds

A mix of both equity and debt assets. Designed to offer a balanced approach between risk and return.

Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)

What is a SIP?

A SIP allows you to invest a fixed amount regularly (monthly or quarterly) in a mutual fund scheme. It instills financial discipline and eliminates the need to time the market.

  • Rupee Cost Averaging: Buy more units when prices are low, fewer when high.
  • Power of Compounding: Early and regular investing yields potential long-term growth.
  • Flexibility: Start, pause, or modify mandates as per your capacity.

SIP vs Lumpsum

While SIPs are ideal for regular salaried investors seeking to build wealth over time, lumpsum investments are suitable when you have a large surplus amount ready to be deployed into the markets.
